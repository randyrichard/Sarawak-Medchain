## What their reply told us, and what it did not

**They will not choose your SWEC.** "Company are responsible to decide/determine which SWEC
is suitable for the business. If you are unable to find the exact SWEC, you may try to find
the nearest SWEC related to the scope." That is a firm position, not an oversight, so naming
your own choice (as this reply does) is the correct response and shows you followed the
instruction.

**They did not answer the Sarawak question.** Nothing in their reply addresses ROB, SSM, or
East Malaysia. It needs asking again, alone, which is why this email raises nothing else.

**They confirmed Bumiputera applies.** "Should the company did not fulfil the minimum
Bumiputera requirement which will affect the non-compliance of the SWEC applied (minimum 51%
or 30% Bumiputera)".

## The SWEC decision, and why 13101600S

Checked against the current list of 20 April 2023, which supersedes the August 2022 edition
I had used earlier. Both candidates are unchanged between the two editions.

| | **13101600S** Software Licence Leasing | **13101400S** Software Development Services |
|---|---|---|
| Description | leasing/rental of software licences | design, development, prototyping, maintenance of software products |
| Class | General | General |
| Minimum Technical Requirement | None | None |
| Bumiputera, self-operated | **30%** | 51% |
| Fit to SafeChain | a subscription to use software | describes a development house building software for clients |

**13101600S is the better answer on both grounds.** It describes what you actually sell, and
it carries the lower Bumiputera threshold. 13101400S describes a contract development
business, which is not what MedChain Enterprise is.

Neither is a perfect description of subscription software for safety management. That is
unavoidable: the list has no SaaS category, and PETRONAS has told you to choose the nearest.
You can add further SWECs later to an existing licence or registration.

## Bumiputera: cleared

Every one of the 56 Digital & ICT codes requires at least 30% Bumiputera participation and
none is set at 0%, so this could have closed the route on its own. It does not.

Randy is Bumiputera (Iban, Sarawak). MedChain Enterprise is a sole proprietorship wholly
owned by him, so the business is 100% Bumiputera at equity, management and employment. That
clears the 30% threshold on 13101600S, the 51% threshold on 13101400S, and in fact the
requirement on every Digital & ICT SWEC including the 51% ones.

**Have the evidence ready before applying**, since PETRONAS asks for it: a birth certificate
stating race, or a confirmation letter from the Native Court or Majlis Adat Istiadat Sarawak.

## One gate left

**Can a Sarawak-registered sole proprietorship apply at all?** Still unanswered, and now the
only thing standing between the business and an application. This email asks it again.

If the answer is no, the fix is a Sdn Bhd, and that route is fully open: a Sdn Bhd wholly
owned by a Bumiputera founder is 100% Bumiputera and satisfies every SWEC threshold. The cost
is the paid-up capital, RM10,000 for registration (downstream only) or RM100,000 for a
licence (upstream and downstream), which must genuinely be in the company.

Neither question affects the iG@P Technology Marketplace submission, which is already lodged
and needs no licence.
