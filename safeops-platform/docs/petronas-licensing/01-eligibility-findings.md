# PETRONAS licensing — verified findings and eligibility position

**Prepared 14 October 2026.** Sources are official PETRONAS publications, cited inline.
Everything below is marked as verified, unconfirmed, or a decision you must make. Nothing
here is an assurance that an application will succeed.

---

## 1. The headline

**Your present business structure appears not to be an eligible applicant type.** This is
stated twice in the current General Guidelines, and it is the finding that governs
everything else in your strategy.

> "Registration of Business (ROB) — Registered with the Companies Commission of Malaysia
> (CCM) or Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia (SSM) as Sole Proprietorship and Partnership.
> **ROB is only applicable for West Malaysia.**"
> — *PETRONAS License and Registration General Guidelines v15.0, effective April 2026, p.6*

And again in the document checklist:

> "d) Sole Proprietorship and Partnership (**Note: ROB only applicable for West Malaysia**)."
> — *same document, p.24, Appendix 2*

You are registered in **Miri, Sarawak**. Two separate problems follow:

1. **Geography.** ROB — the sole proprietorship and partnership category — is restricted to
   West Malaysia. Sarawak is East Malaysia.
2. **Registry.** PETRONAS requires registration with CCM/SSM. Your registration number,
   `MRI/BNR/718/2026`, is a Sarawak Business Names Register number issued under the state
   registry, not an SSM number. Sarawak sole proprietorships do not register with SSM.

The word "Sarawak" appears in the guidelines only once, in the Agency Appointment appendix,
as a *coverage location* for an appointed agent — never as an accepted registration
jurisdiction.

**What this does not mean.** It does not mean you are shut out. It means the licence-first
strategy, as currently drafted, cannot be executed by the entity you have today. The cheapest
correct next step is to ask PETRONAS directly, in writing, before spending anything — draft
at `02-email-swec-enquiry.md`.

## 2. Licence and registration are different things

| | **Licence** | **Registration** |
|---|---|---|
| Sectors | Upstream **and** downstream | Downstream only |
| Who you may supply | PACs, PETRONAS subsidiaries, offshore fabrication contractors, MISC | PETRONAS Group companies only |
| Legal basis | Petroleum Development Act 1974 and Petroleum Regulations 1974 | A PETRONAS requirement, not statute |
| Minimum paid-up capital | **RM100,000** | **RM10,000** |
| Validity | 3 years | 3 years |

*Sources: [PETRONAS Licensing & Procurement, Malaysia](https://www.petronas.com/suppliers/licensing-procurement-malaysia); General Guidelines v15.0, pp.5–6.*

This distinction matters commercially. A **registration** is ten times cheaper in capital and
is sufficient if your buyers are PETRONAS Group companies. A **licence** is required to supply
the upstream sector, and upstream operators are where permit-to-work and HSE software is most
obviously needed.

## 3. The four general requirements

From the General Guidelines v15.0, p.6 — a vendor must:

1. Be **locally incorporated**
2. Meet the **minimum paid-up capital** requirement (RM100,000 licence / RM10,000 registration)
3. Have a **positive net worth**
4. Be licensed or registered in **at least one approved SWEC**

Net worth is evidenced by the latest **Audited Financial Statement**, submitted annually
within eight months of financial year end (p.14). A newly incorporated company has no
trading history, which is a question to put to PETRONAS rather than assume either way.

## 4. Which SWEC — not yet answerable

The SWEC list is published separately. There is a **Digital & ICT** category, and three codes
are plausible candidates. None of them describes "subscription software for workplace safety
management", so **the correct code must be confirmed by PETRONAS, not chosen by us.**

| Code | Category | Strategic / General | Licence or registration | MTR | Bumiputera required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **13101400S** | Software Development Services | General | L/R | **None** | **51%** (self-operated) |
| **13101600S** | Software License Leasing | General | L/R | **None** | **30%** (self-operated) / 51% (agent) |
| 13141100S | ICT Consultancy | Strategic | L/R | **2 MTRs apply** | 30% (self-operated) / 51% (agent) |

*Source: [Updated SWEC List, 29 August 2022](https://www.petronas.com/themes/custom/petronas/pdf/New-SWEC-List-29-August-2022.pdf), rows 133, 135, 147.*

Two observations worth your attention:

- **13101600S, "Software License Leasing"**, is described as *"the provision of
  leasing/rental services for Software License Leasing"*. A SaaS subscription is arguably
  closer to this than to software development. It also carries the **lower Bumiputera
  threshold (30%)** and no Minimum Technical Requirement. If PETRONAS accepts it, it is the
  easier of the two routes.
- **This list is dated August 2022.** A newer version may exist. Confirm before relying on
  any code above.

## 5. Bumiputera requirement — a decision point you must verify

Every candidate SWEC above carries a Bumiputera participation requirement of 30% or 51%.
The guidelines set the requirement **per SWEC** at 100%, 51%, 30% or 0%, applied across four
levels — equity, board of directors, management and employment — with the required
composition varying by company type (v15.0, p.9).

**I have not assumed your status either way.** For a Sarawak native, Bumiputera status is
evidenced by a birth certificate stating race or a confirmation letter from the relevant
authority; the guidelines name *Pejabat Hal Ehwal Anak Negeri Sabah* for Sabah and "any other
applicable or relevant authority" elsewhere. For Sarawak this is ordinarily the Native Court
or Majlis Adat Istiadat Sarawak. **Confirm what evidence you can produce before applying.**

If you cannot meet the Bumiputera threshold for a given SWEC, that SWEC is closed to you, and
the question becomes whether a 0% SWEC fits the product — which again only PETRONAS can answer.

## 6. Eligibility checklist

### A. Verified from official sources

- Licence covers upstream and downstream; registration covers downstream only.
- Minimum paid-up capital: RM100,000 (licence), RM10,000 (registration).
- Positive net worth required; evidenced by audited financial statements annually.
- Must hold at least one approved SWEC.
- Validity three years; applications via the PETRONAS Licensing Management System (PLMS).
- Applicant types are ROC (Sdn Bhd / Berhad), ROB (sole proprietorship and partnership) and
  Professional Bodies.
- **ROB is restricted to West Malaysia.**
- A Digital & ICT SWEC family exists, with software development and software licence leasing
  categories, both General, both without Minimum Technical Requirements.
- Those categories carry Bumiputera requirements of 51% and 30% respectively.

### B. Awaiting confirmation from PETRONAS

- Whether a **Sarawak-registered sole proprietorship** can apply at all, given both the West
  Malaysia restriction and the state registry question.
- Whether **any** route exists for a Sarawak enterprise short of incorporating a Sdn Bhd.
- Which SWEC covers **subscription software for workplace safety management**, and whether
  13101600S or 13101400S is the right one.
- Whether a newer SWEC list supersedes the August 2022 edition.
- How **net worth and audited accounts** are assessed for a newly incorporated company with
  no trading history.
- Current application and annual fees under v15.0.
- Whether **VDP InnoTech** requires licence or registration as a precondition, or is a
  separate pathway — see `03-email-vdp-innotech.md`.

### C. Information and documents you must provide

- Confirmation of your **Bumiputera status**, and the evidence available for it.
- A decision on **licence or registration** — this sets the capital requirement at RM100,000
  or RM10,000.
- A decision on whether you are willing to **incorporate a Sdn Bhd**.
- Your SSM or Sarawak registry certificate.
- Financial statements, once an eligible entity exists.
- Beneficial ownership declaration.

## 7. Contingency, if the enterprise is ineligible

In increasing order of cost. **Do not spend on step 3 until step 1 has answered.**

1. **Ask first — costs nothing.** Send the enquiry at `02-email-swec-enquiry.md`. It asks the
   two questions that decide everything: can a Sarawak sole proprietorship apply, and which
   SWEC covers this product. Until these are answered, any spending is speculative.

2. **Pursue pilots that need no PETRONAS status.** Nothing stops you selling SafeChain to
   Sarawak contractors, construction firms, marine and engineering businesses today. Most of
   them are not PETRONAS vendors and do not require you to be one. A completed pilot with
   real evidence makes every later application stronger, and it is the only route that
   produces revenue. **This is the recommended parallel track regardless of the licensing
   answer.**

3. **Incorporate a Sdn Bhd — only if PETRONAS confirms it is necessary.** A Sdn Bhd is an ROC
   applicant, registered with SSM, with no West Malaysia restriction. Paid-up capital of
   RM10,000 opens registration; RM100,000 opens a licence. Incorporation itself is
   inexpensive; the paid-up capital is the real commitment, and it is money that must
   genuinely be in the company.

**On sequencing.** Your stated strategy is licence first, then pilot. On these findings that
order is difficult: the licence route is blocked by your current structure, while the pilot
route is open today and needs nobody's permission. I would run them in parallel — send the
enquiry this week, and keep approaching Sarawak operators while you wait. A pilot in hand
also answers the "positive net worth" and "trading history" questions that a brand-new Sdn
Bhd cannot.
