# PILOT FEEDBACK

A 30-day structure for finding out whether SafeOps is worth paying for.

The purpose is not to be told it is good. A pilot that ends with "yes, very nice" and no
renewal has failed, and it usually fails quietly at week two when people stop logging in.
Everything here is designed to catch that early.

---

## The two things that actually matter

Everything else is detail:

1. **Did anyone use it without being asked?**
2. **Did it tell them something they did not already know?**

Usage without prompting is the only honest signal of value. Someone filling in SafeOps
because you asked them to is doing you a favour, not using a product.

---

## What to measure yourself, weekly

Don't ask them for these — read them from the system, so the numbers are not filtered
through what somebody wants you to hear.

| Signal | Where | Healthy sign |
|---|---|---|
| Users invited vs activated | Administration → Invitations | Most accept within 48h |
| Distinct users signing in | Administration → Users, last login | Grows past week 1 |
| Incidents reported | Incidents | At least weekly, unprompted |
| Incidents reaching closed | Incident board | At least one full lifecycle |
| Permits raised | Permits | Matches their real high-risk work |
| Actions overdue | Dashboard | Some — zero means nobody is using it |
| Reports generated | Reports → History | Any use at all is a strong signal |

**A quiet week two is the warning sign.** If reporting stops after the novelty, ask why
immediately rather than waiting for the final review.

---

## DAY 1 — after onboarding

Ask while they are still at the screen. Five minutes, verbally.

1. Was anything confusing in the first ten minutes?
2. Did the invitation email arrive, and was it clear what to do?
3. Is there anything you expected to see and didn't?
4. On a scale of 1–5, how easy was it to get started?

## WEEK 1 — usability

1. How many of your team have actually signed in?
2. What did you try to do that you couldn't work out?
3. Did you report a real incident, or only a test one?
4. What are you still doing on paper or WhatsApp?
5. Ratings 1–5: **ease of use**, **speed**, **clarity of the dashboard**

## WEEK 2 — the workflows

1. Did you take an incident all the way to closed? What stopped you if not?
2. Did the investigation steps match how you actually investigate?
3. Did you raise a permit for a real job?
4. Did the right person get told at the right time?
5. Is anything in the workflow more work than your current method?
6. Ratings 1–5: **incident management**, **permit management**, **corrective actions**

Question 5 is the important one. A safety system that is *more* work than a spreadsheet
gets abandoned no matter how good it looks.

## WEEK 3 — reliability and adoption

1. Has anything been slow, broken, or logged you out unexpectedly?
2. Who is using it daily, weekly, and not at all?
3. Has anyone outside HSE used it — supervisors, maintenance, management?
4. Has it changed a conversation, a meeting, or a decision?
5. Ratings 1–5: **reliability**, **speed**, **usefulness**

## WEEK 4 — the commercial conversation

This is the one that decides everything. Do it in person if you can.

1. What problem did SafeOps actually solve?
2. Which feature was most useful?
3. Which feature was confusing or unused?
4. What are you still doing manually that you hoped it would handle?
5. What is missing that would stop you rolling it out to another site?
6. Was the dashboard useful, or did you skip it?
7. Did management ask to see anything from it?
8. How often was it used in week 4 compared with week 1?
9. **What would make your team use SafeOps every day?**
10. **What would make you pay for it?**
11. **What price would feel reasonable for a site this size?**
12. **What would stop you renewing?**
13. Would you recommend it to another company? Who?
14. Rating 1–5: **overall satisfaction**
15. If we ended the pilot tomorrow, what would you go back to — and would that feel worse?

Question 15 is the real willingness-to-pay test. If going back feels like a relief, no
price is right.

---

## How to ask about price without anchoring

Do **not** open with RM10,000. Ask 11 first and let them say a number.

If they ask you directly:

> "I'm working that out from pilots like this one. What I need to know from you is whether
> it is worth anything — and if so, roughly what a site this size could justify."

If their number is far below yours, that is the most valuable thing you learn all month.
It is worth more than a polite yes.

---

## Reading the answers

| What they say | What it means |
|---|---|
| "That's exactly the problem we have" | Keep going. This is the sentence you are hunting |
| "It's very nice" | Nothing. Ask question 15 |
| "We'd need X before rolling out" | X is your roadmap. Write it down verbatim |
| "We stopped using it in week 3" | Find out precisely which day and what happened |
| Nobody outside HSE logged in | The product is a departmental tool, not a platform — that limits price |

## After the pilot

Write down, in their own words:

1. The one sentence describing what SafeOps did for them
2. The one thing that nearly stopped them using it
3. The price they named
4. Whether they want to continue, and on what terms

Three pilots' worth of those four answers is enough to build the commercial package on
evidence instead of hope. Until then, `PILOT_PRICING_NOTES.md` stays a set of placeholders.
