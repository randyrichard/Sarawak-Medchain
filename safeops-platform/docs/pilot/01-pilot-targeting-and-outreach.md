# First pilot: who to approach, and what to send

The aim is not a big logo. It is **one site, finished, with numbers at the end**. Every
document we have written for PETRONAS has the same empty column: no customers, no track
record, no measured value. One completed pilot fills all three, and it is the only track that
also produces revenue.

---

## 1. What a good first pilot looks like

Pick for **likelihood of finishing**, not for prestige.

| | Good first pilot | Avoid for the first one |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 50 to 500 people | Under 30 (paper genuinely works), over 2,000 (procurement committee) |
| Sites | 2 to 5 | A single site, or thirty |
| Decision maker | Owner, or an HSE manager who reports to the owner | A procurement department, or a committee |
| Current system | Paper, Excel, WhatsApp, shared folders | An incumbent enterprise system mid-contract |
| Work type | Hot work, confined space, work at height, lifting, contractor crews | Low-hazard office operations |
| Distance | You can drive there | Anywhere you cannot reach the same day |
| Procurement | Buys software on the owner's signature | Needs a tender, a vendor licence, or a 90 day legal review |

**The single most important filter is who signs.** A 120-person contractor whose owner answers
his own phone can say yes in a week. A major fabricator will take a year and a tender, and
will probably require the PETRONAS licence you do not yet have.

**Deliberately avoid PETRONAS-licensed vendors for the first pilot.** They are the companies
most likely to ask whether you are licensed, which is the one question you cannot currently
answer. Target firms that sell to industry but are not themselves in the PETRONAS vendor
chain: building contractors, civil and infrastructure, plantation operations, marine
services, workshops and fabrication shops serving the oil and gas industry at second tier.

## 2. Where to find them, warmest first

**Your own cohort at Fajar International College.** This is your real advantage and you
should use it before anything else. Your classmates on the Diploma in OSH are working, or
have worked, at exactly these companies. A classmate who introduces you to their HSE manager
converts at a rate cold email never will. Ask ten of them who their employer's safety officer
is and whether they would make an introduction. That is one afternoon's work and it is worth
more than any list I can build.

**Your lecturers.** OSH lecturers at a Miri college know the local HSE community, usually sit
on industry panels, and are often consulted by employers. One lecturer's recommendation is
worth fifty emails.

**NIOSH and DOSH Sarawak events.** Safety officers attend these. So do you, as a student.
Go, and leave with names rather than brochures.

**MOGSC corporate members directory**, at [mogsc.org/corporate-members](https://mogsc.org/corporate-members/).
Filter for Sarawak addresses. Treat it as a starting point, not a verified list.

**LinkedIn, searched by job title rather than company.** Search "HSE Manager Miri", "Safety
Officer Bintulu", "HSE Executive Sarawak". You are looking for the person, not the firm, and
the person's profile tells you the firm.

**Sarawak Chamber of Commerce and Industry, and the Miri Chamber.** Member directories, and
events where owners rather than employees attend.

### On names I found but cannot verify

## 3. Cold first-contact email

Short on purpose. An HSE manager reads email between site walks.

**Subject:** Safety software built in Miri, free pilot at one site

Dear [name],

I am Randy Richard, based in Miri. I have built a workplace safety system called SafeChain,
and I am looking for one company to run it at a single site for 30 days, at no cost, so that
it can be proven in a real operation rather than in a demonstration.

It covers incidents and investigations, corrective actions, permits to work, inspections, and
training and certificate expiry. It produces your monthly safety report as a finished
document rather than a morning's work in Excel.

You can look at it before you reply: **safechain.schain.workers.dev**, and sign in with any
of the demonstration roles shown on the page. It carries sample data and there is nothing to
install.

What I am asking for is one site, 30 days, and a little of your time at the start and the
end. What you get is the system set up and configured by me in person, at no cost, with no
obligation afterwards.

If that is worth fifteen minutes, I can come to your office.

Randy Richard
Founder, MedChain Enterprise, Miri

## 5. What you are actually offering

Agree this before you start, in writing, even informally. A pilot without a defined end does
not end.

**Scope.** One site. The modules they choose, which will usually be incidents, corrective
actions and permits.

**Duration.** 30 days from the day their data is loaded, not from the day they said yes.

**What you do.** Set up the site, load their employees and contractors, configure their permit
types, train their people on site, and remain reachable throughout.

**What they do.** Use it for real work rather than in parallel with paper, and give you
fifteen minutes at the start and thirty at the end.

**Cost.** Nothing, for the pilot.

**What you ask for in return.** Not money. Permission to say they piloted it, a short written
comment you may quote, and their agreement that you may measure and cite the figures below.

**What gets measured**, agreed with them at the start and taken from a baseline before you
deploy:

- time to produce the monthly safety report, before and during
- time to retrieve a specific safety record
- proportion of corrective actions closed by their due date
- proportion of permits closed correctly against those issued
- how many of their people actually used it

**How it ends.** Either they continue on a paid subscription, or they stop and keep an export
of their own data. Say the second part out loud when you propose it. It is what makes the
first part believable.

## 6. Two things to be honest with yourself about

**A free pilot attracts people who do not value it.** The protection is not charging money,
it is asking for commitment: a named owner on their side, agreed dates, and a scheduled
closing meeting. If they will not give you those, they are not going to use it, and a dead
pilot is worse than no pilot because it costs you six weeks and gives you nothing to cite.

**Ten approaches is not a test.** Expect most not to reply. The number that matters is how
many you get in front of, and the only reliable way to raise it is the warm route in section
2. Your college network is worth more than your email list.
