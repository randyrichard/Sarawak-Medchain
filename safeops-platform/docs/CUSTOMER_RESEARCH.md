# Customer Research — Safety Officer Interview

**Date captured:** 2026-07-27
**Method:** structured Q&A plus WhatsApp follow-up
**Participants:** 1 Safety Officer working a multi-contractor SIMOPS site (oil & gas /
construction, Sarawak). Toolbox meetings of ~350 personnel.

> **Sample size is n=1.** This is strong qualitative signal from a practitioner, not
> validated demand. Findings below are quoted or closely paraphrased; anything marked
> *(inference)* is our interpretation, not something the interviewee said. Do not present
> this as multi-customer validation.

---

## Their day

- Mass toolbox meeting with contractors, **~350 personnel**
- Early worksite walk to identify hazards — **simultaneous operations** with several
  service companies at once
- Hot work, confined space, LOTO, working at height — *"Each job had their own checklist
  need to make sure to be followed."*
- **Daily PTW audit**: *"Make sure all Permit to work valid for work and in compliance and
  site PTW audit."* Some jobs need a **gas test before the job starts for the day**
- Verify **JSA** is correct for the task; **create JHA** for the work
- **Report daily UA/UC** (unsafe act / unsafe condition), plus coaching conversations
- Check **SDS, USECHH, chemical labelling, waste management**
- Attend client meetings

**Most time spent:** *"Monitoring workers at site to ensure they are following the proper
procedure. No shortcut."*

**Daily paperwork:** *"PTW audit, UAUC reporting"*

## Pain points (verbatim)

| # | Pain point | Quote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | **Near misses are under-reported** | *"workers often dont report near misses bcs of too much paperwork, takes too long, they forget, they think its unimp[ortant]"* — and on why: *"Maybe ndai award or they tend to forget to report"* (no recognition, or they forget) |
| P2 | **Past incident history is hard to find** | *"past incident histories. Some companies make it confidential."* |
| P3 | **Open follow-up items get forgotten** | *"i used to forget following up open item for unsafe condition. Until SAIL help me to record required to be follow up."* |
| P4 | **SAIL is load-bearing** | Asked what happens if SAIL disappeared tomorrow: *"create something to replace SAIL."* |
| P5 | **Accident Investigation report takes longest to prepare** | Listed first when asked which reports take longest |
| P6 | **Still on Excel, email and paper** | Q: *"so today kitk agi ngena excel, email and paper files ka?"* A: *"Agik. Mesti simpan semua document."* (Still. Must keep all documents.) |
| P7 | **Hardest emotional load** — fatality/LTI reports | *"When I need to make incident / accident report for the workers who can't go back to work or when the person lost their live."* |

**Reports named as slow/needed:** Accident Investigation report; **LTI-free days**, first
aid case, medical treatment case, hazard reporting, property damage. Also wants *"Latest
incident worldwide so it could be shared to the crew."*

## Current workflow (as described)

**Accident reporting**
1. Notify superintendent / site supervisor → escalates to Project Manager + HSE Manager
2. **Interview and record all witness statements**
3. Create **early initial incident report** → superintendent reviews → submit to client
4. **Photographs required** for the report
5. **All reports submitted to DOSH by January each year**

**Inspections** — record: location · hazard/non-conformance · recommended corrective
action · inform person responsible · **get a completion date if not settled that day**

**Corrective actions (SAIL — Safety Action Item List)**
- Person in charge must be informed
- **All open items require a target completion date**
- Recorded in SAIL

**Follow-up when someone doesn't complete**
- **End of each week, SAIL is sent out to all respective department heads**
- **Weekly meeting with all HODs** to discuss target dates and confirm completion

**Audit prep** — *"auditors will ask for the procedure. Just need to know how and where to
get it."*

## What was NOT said (guard against over-reading)

- Asked *"what wastes the most time?"* they answered about loving the job — **no time-waste
  was named.** Do not invent one.
- Asked *"if you could remove one task forever?"* they answered philosophically about
  barriers and Deepwater Horizon — **they did not ask for any task to be removed.** The
  actual message was the opposite: every barrier must be followed, no shortcuts.
- Asked *"which tasks do you dislike most?"* they said they dislike none.
- **No pain point was raised about permits being hard to manage** — permits appear as
  daily work (PTW audit, gas test), not as a complaint.
- Nothing was said about dashboards, mobile devices, search, or PDF export. Improvements in
  those areas are *(inference)* from the workflow, not stated needs.

## Design implications

| Pain | Implication |
| --- | --- |
| P1 near-miss friction | Reporting must take seconds, work on a phone at the work face, and require the minimum viable fields. Recognition/acknowledgement is worth testing *(inference — they said "maybe no award", not a request)* |
| P3 + P4 SAIL | The action tracker must own: owner, **target completion date**, open/closed state, **weekly digest to department heads**, and a **HOD meeting view** grouped by department |
| P5 investigation report | Witness statements and photos are first-class inputs, not attachments bolted on; output must be a submittable report |
| P2 incident history | Past incidents must be searchable by the officer who needs them |
| P6 Excel/email/paper | Export must exist for everything, because documents still have to be kept and circulated |
| Daily PTW audit + gas test | Already covered by the Permit to Work module (commit 8634123) |
| DOSH January submission | Annual statutory export is a real deadline, not a nice-to-have |
